# Galapagos Islands Travel Guide

## Overview

The Galapagos Islands are a living laboratory of evolution, scattered across the
Pacific some 1,000 km off the coast of Ecuador. Famous for fearless wildlife —
giant tortoises, marine iguanas, and blue-footed boobies — this volcanic
archipelago offers world-class snorkeling, hiking, and once-in-a-lifetime
encounters with nature.

## Highlights

- Snorkel with sea lions, penguins, and sea turtles
- See giant tortoises in the wild on Santa Cruz
- Walk among nesting seabird colonies on Española and Genovesa
- Cruise or stay on the islands with expert naturalist guides

## Things to Do

From island-hopping day tours to multi-day cruises, the Galapagos rewards both
active travelers and those seeking a relaxed pace. Snorkeling, kayaking, and
guided nature walks are the headline activities, alongside visits to the
Charles Darwin Research Station.

## Best Time to Visit

The Galapagos is a year-round destination. The warm season (December–May) brings
calmer, warmer water ideal for snorkeling, while the cool season (June–November)
offers more active wildlife and nutrient-rich seas.

## How to Get There

Fly from Quito or Guayaquil in mainland Ecuador to either Baltra (Santa Cruz)
or San Cristobal. From there, transfers connect you to your cruise or island
hotel.

## Good to Know

A Galapagos National Park entrance fee (currently $200) and an Ingala transit
card ($20) are paid on arrival. Pack reef-safe sunscreen, and follow your
guide's instructions to protect the fragile ecosystem.

## FAQ

### Do I need a cruise to visit the Galapagos?

No. You can base yourself in island hotels and take day tours, though cruises
reach more remote sites with abundant wildlife.

### Is the Galapagos suitable for families?

Yes — many operators offer family departures, and the wildlife encounters are a
hit with children and adults alike.
