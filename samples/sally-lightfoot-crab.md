# Sally Lightfoot Crab

## How to Spot Them

Look for intense, neon red and yellow shells contrasting sharply against the
dark basalt lava rock of the shoreline. Their color alone makes them
unmistakable.

- Juveniles: Jet-black camouflage coloration to hide from coastal shorebirds.
- Adults: Vivid red-orange shells with pale blue undersides and yellow accents.

## A Splash of Color on the Coasts

These ubiquitous crabs line nearly every rocky shoreline across the archipelago.
They play a vital cleanup role by scraping algae off raw rock and picking ticks
and parasites off resting marine iguanas.

Why you'll love finding them: they move with incredible speed, leaping from rock
to rock directly over incoming ocean spray.

## Lifestyle & Traits

- Diet & Behavior: Highly active during low-tide cycles. They gather in loose,
  multi-age social clusters to feed on tide-pool algae before the waves submerge
  the rocks again.
- Acrobatic Agility: Unlike standard crabs that crawl sideways, Sally Lightfoots
  can run in all four directions and leap over gaps up to a foot wide to escape
  predators.

## Quick Traveler FAQ

### What is the likelihood of spotting one on a standard cruise?

Extremely high — close to guaranteed. They live on virtually every rocky
shoreline, so almost any coastal landing will put you near them.

### Are they seasonal, or can they be seen all year?

They can be seen year-round. There is no season; they are active on the rocks
throughout the year.

### Is viewing this species included across all tour tiers?

Yes. Because they're so widespread along the coast, every Galapagos itinerary
that includes shore landings will encounter them.
