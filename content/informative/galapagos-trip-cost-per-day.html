How Much Does a Galápagos Trip Cost Per Day?

Published 16 July 2026 · Reviewed by Juan Magallanes, Galápagos Travel Advisor · Galapagos Islands.Travel editorial team

Plan on roughly $150–$700 per person per day land-based (hotels plus day tours) or $445–$2,193 per day on a cruise (median about $907). The cruise-vs-land choice moves your budget more than anything else. On top of either style, everyone pays a one-off $200 park fee, a about $20 transit card, and about $400–$500 in mainland flights.

Fixed costs (paid once, not daily): $200 park fee · about $20 INGALA card · about $400–$500 mainland flights

What fixed costs does everyone pay, whatever the trip?

Three costs hit every traveller no matter how you visit — and none is a daily rate. Think of them as gateway costs: you pay them once, then your daily spend begins.

  • Galápagos National Park entrance fee — $200 for most foreign adults, paid in cash on arrival. It rose on 1 August 2024, the first increase since 1998; children, Ecuadorians and CAN/MERCOSUR nationals pay reduced rates.
  • INGALA transit control card — about $20 per traveller. As of 2025 you buy it online in advance; hold on to it for departure.
  • Mainland round-trip airfare — about $400–$500 from Quito or Guayaquil to Baltra or San Cristóbal, about 3 hours each way. There are no passenger boats from the mainland, so fly — and arrive a day early.

All three sit outside the per-day ranges below, on a cruise and on land alike. Budget them separately so the daily figures stay honest.

Cruise vs land-based: the single biggest per-day lever

The one decision that moves your daily cost most is where you sleep: a hotel with day tours, or a cabin aboard a cruise. Here are the honest ranges — both drawn from our own data.

Land-based, per person per day (about $150–$700)

A land-based trip runs about $150–$700 per person per day — budget hostels plus shared day tours at the low end, high-end hotels plus private guiding at the top. This reflects Galapagos Islands.Travel advisory data from our own booking experience.

The trade-off is reach: day-trips from the inhabited islands get you to roughly one-third of the archipelago's visitor sites.

Cruise, per person per day (about $445–$2,193, median about $907)

Across the fleet, cruises run about $445–$2,193 per person per day, with a median around $907 — from 256 price points across 58 vessels, taking the cheapest non-charter cabin divided by trip length on 2026/2027 rates. By category:

  • 3-star: about $445–$928 (median $640)
  • 4-star: about $505–$1,170 (median $825)
  • 5-star: about $520–$2,193 (median $1,189)

(2-star has only four usable data points — too thin to quote as a band.)

Why the star tier doesn't track price — and what money actually buys

Star category is not a clean price ladder. The 5-star median ($1,189) sits above the 4-star median ($825), but the 4-star maximum ($1,170) overlaps deep into 5-star territory.

Here is the part that saves first-timers money: a pricier cruise does not reach better islands. Nearly all boats visit nearly all islands, so the itinerary is not what you are paying for. Every island is reachable on a 5-day-or-longer cruise; the only sites off-limits to a 4-day-or-shorter program are Fernandina and the west side of Isabela (Elizabeth Bay, Urbina Bay, Tagus Cove, Punta Vicente Roca). The shortest cruise is 4 days, and a 4-day cruise can absolutely be top-tier.

What the extra money buys is the yacht's full experience — service, guiding, cuisine, cabins and crew — not a different map.

What's NOT included, even on an "all-inclusive" cruise?

An all-inclusive fare covers your cabin, meals, guided landings and onboard activities — but several real costs sit outside it. Budget for these on top:

  • The three fixed gateway costs — $200 park fee, about $20 transit card, about $400–$500 mainland flights.
  • International flights to Quito or Guayaquil, separate from the mainland-to-island hop.
  • Travel insurance, which we always recommend for a date- and flight-constrained trip.
  • Crew tips, alcohol and wetsuit hire — commonly excluded from the base fare.
  • Mainland hotel nights — you should arrive a day early, and that night is on you.

How can I spend less per day?

There are honest levers here, and none of them costs you the wildlife:

  • Base yourself on land. Hotels plus day tours (about $150–$700/day) undercut most cruises; accept that you'll reach about a third of the sites.
  • Choose a shorter or lower-tier cruise. A 3-star cabin (about $445–$928/day) reaches the same headline islands as pricier tiers.
  • Travel in the cool season or take a late-availability cabin. Both can trim the per-day rate — pick them for price and availability, not because any month is inherently better.
  • Share a cabin to avoid the solo supplement. Solo travellers often pay a single-occupancy premium; sharing, or picking an operator that waives it on select departures, removes that penalty.
  • Book your international and mainland flights separately rather than through a packaged upsell.

Frequently asked questions about Galápagos trip costs

How much does a Galápagos trip cost per day?

Roughly $150–$700 per person per day land-based (hotel plus day tours) or about $445–$2,193 per day on a cruise, with a fleet median around $907. On top of either, everyone pays a one-off $200 park fee, a about $20 transit card and about $400–$500 in mainland flights.

Is a Galápagos cruise or a land-based trip cheaper per day?

Land-based is cheaper day-for-day: about $150–$700 versus about $445–$2,193 on a cruise. The trade-off is reach — a land base gets you to about one-third of the visitor sites, while a 5-day-plus cruise can reach them all.

What is the Galápagos National Park entrance fee?

$200 for most foreign adults, paid in cash on arrival. It rose on 1 August 2024 — the first increase since 1998. Children, Ecuadorians and CAN/MERCOSUR nationals pay reduced rates. It is a one-off gateway cost, not a daily one.

Are flights and park fees included in a Galápagos cruise price?

No. Mainland flights (about $400–$500), the $200 park fee and the about $20 INGALA transit card are extra on essentially every cruise fare, and so are international flights to Ecuador. Always budget these three fixed costs separately.

How much does a week in the Galápagos cost?

For a 7-day trip, plan roughly about $2,070 budget land-based, about $7,020 mid-range cruise (fleet median) or about $11,170 high-end yacht per person — each figure includes the $200 park fee, about $20 card and about $450 flights. Your real total shifts with cabin, season and operator.

Does a more expensive cruise visit better islands?

No. Nearly all boats reach nearly all islands, so price buys the onboard experience — service, guiding, cuisine and cabins — not a better map. Every island is reachable on a 5-day-plus cruise; only Fernandina and west Isabela need more than a 4-day program.

What extra costs are not included on an all-inclusive Galápagos cruise?

Beyond the fare: international flights to Ecuador, the about $400–$500 mainland hop, the $200 park fee, the about $20 transit card, travel insurance, crew tips, alcohol, wetsuit hire and any mainland hotel nights before or after the cruise. Budget these on top.

How can I do the Galápagos on a budget?

Base yourself on land (about $150–$700/day), pick a shorter or lower-tier cruise (a 3-star cabin reaches the same headline islands), travel in the cool season or take a late cabin for price, share a cabin to dodge the solo supplement, and book flights separately.

Is there a solo traveller supplement in the Galápagos?

Often, yes — solo travellers can pay a single-occupancy premium on top of the per-day rate. It is not universal: some operators waive or reduce it on select departures, and sharing a cabin removes it entirely. Tell us your dates and we'll find the ones that don't penalise solo travellers.
